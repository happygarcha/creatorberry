**Description**
```
On Thursday, November 12, 2026, Outlook and other Microsoft 365 services stopped working for millions of people. What happened, and why did it take so long to fix? A cartoon explainer.

What we know so far:
- Error reports spiked around 12:39 PM ET; more than 129,000 were logged on Downdetector by 1:09 PM ET (Variety).
- Features that depend on Exchange Online, Microsoft's email engine, were hit: Outlook, Teams calendars, Copilot and parts of SharePoint. Teams chat and calls mostly kept working.
- Microsoft said it was a technical issue, not related to cyber attacks or AI (ABC News).
- Microsoft's preliminary post-incident report (as published by Office Watch) points to three things lining up: a hidden bug in a July fix for its front-door gateway servers that its lab tests missed, extra load after Teams traffic moved onto the same gateway about 15 hours earlier, and nearly 14% of server capacity already out of service. Servers locked up one after another; more than 80% stopped responding in under two hours.
- Recovery was slow because restarted servers were swamped by waiting traffic and the usual undo did not help. Engineers rolled the code back and restored servers in batches, Asia Pacific first, then Europe.
- Webmail was back to normal worldwide on Friday morning (ET); Microsoft marked the incident fully restored late Friday after the email backlog was processed. Delayed email was not lost.

The report is preliminary and a final report was still pending, so details may change. This is a simplified cartoon explainer.
```

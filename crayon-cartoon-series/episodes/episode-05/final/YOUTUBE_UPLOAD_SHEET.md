**Description**
```
That water dripping from your air conditioner looks clean. Do not drink it. A cartoon explainer in four parts.

What we covered:
- Part 1: Warm, humid room air hits the cold coils inside the AC and turns into drops (condensation). The drops run into a pan and out the drain pipe.
- Part 2: The AC also pulls moisture out of the air. Dry air can irritate skin, eyes and throat. A comfortable indoor humidity is commonly given as about 30 to 50 percent.
- Part 3: Many room ACs recirculate indoor air. In a closed bedroom, carbon dioxide builds up overnight. Studies found more carbon dioxide in air conditioned bedrooms and less deep sleep when bedroom ventilation is low. Let some fresh air in.
- Part 4: Fresh condensate is almost like distilled water, but it touches the coils, drain pan and pipes on the way out. Germs can build up there, including, in some systems, the bacteria behind Legionnaires disease, and parts can add metals like copper (some coils use lead solder). Use it for plants, not for drinking.

Sources: Slate, university extension Q&A, EPA water-quality guidance, a Singapore bedroom ventilation study, a 2023 bedroom ventilation field study (PubMed). This is a simplified cartoon explainer, not medical advice.
```

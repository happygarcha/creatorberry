**Description**
```
What if the next giant AI data center floated alone in the middle of the Pacific Ocean? A startup called Panthalassa is trying to make that real. A cartoon explainer.

What we found:
- Panthalassa is a public benefit company from Portland, Oregon, founded in 2016. Its name comes from the giant ocean that once surrounded the supercontinent Pangaea.
- Its "nodes" are steel floats reported to be about 85 meters long: an air-filled ball on top and a long tube below. Waves push water up the tube and it falls back through turbines to make electricity, with few moving parts.
- The power is used on board to run AI chips sealed in containers under the surface and cooled by the ocean. Results are sent to land by satellite. The nodes are not anchored and steer themselves.
- In May 2026 it raised $140 million led by Peter Thiel, about $210 million in total; reports put its value near $1 billion.
- It tested prototypes (Ocean-1, Ocean-2) at sea; one report says up to 50 kW. A factory opened in Vancouver, Washington in November 2026. Ocean-3 pilots are planned for the northern Pacific, with commercial nodes planned for 2027.
- The catch: wave energy has a history of failures, there is no independently audited output or uptime data yet, repairs far from shore are hard, and satellite links are slower than cables.

Sources: Panthalassa (panthalassa.com), GeekWire, Interesting Engineering, Wikipedia, OPB, CleanTechnica, Latitude Media. Status of the Ocean-3 deployment was not confirmed as of November 2026. This is a simplified cartoon explainer.
```
